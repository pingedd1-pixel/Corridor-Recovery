The brand is the paper.

Every document we send will be read by a yard owner on a phone, a procurement manager in Gujarat, or a bank officer checking an LC line by line. It has to look like it was made by people who don't make mistakes. Nothing on the page decorates; everything on the page informs.

Name and mark

Corridor Metals — legally, "[numbered co.] B.C. Ltd. dba Corridor Metals". The name says the job: we move metal down corridors that others can't or won't.

The mark is two parallel rules — the corridor — with the wordmark set in the lane and a single copper square: the lot, in transit. It is drawn in type and rules, so it reproduces in Word, email, a PDF footer, or a WhatsApp profile without an image file.

Rules for the mark

  • CORRIDOR in semibold, METALS in light, same size, same line. Never stacked, never in colour.
  • The copper square sits on the left, centred in the lane. It is the only copper on the page besides the words "Corridor Metals" in a signature.
  • Minimum clear space: the height of the lane, all sides.
  • No tagline. Not "global", not "West Coast", not "trusted". The counterparty decides that.

Colour

Ink, paper, aluminium, one copper accent. Three status colours exist because the spec requires every figure to be labelled verified, rough, or unverified — colour is doing a job the reader needs, not a mood.

Type

One family: IBM Plex Sans. Free, installs on Mac, Windows and phones, has real tabular figures so columns of tonnes and dollars line up, and reads as engineered rather than corporate. Fallback when it isn't installed: Arial. Never Calibri, never Times.

Voice

Write like this

  • Lead with the number and the call.
  • Short sentences. Active verbs. "We will book five boxes by 24 October" not "shipment is anticipated".
  • "Unverified", "rough", "as of" are required words, not hedges.
  • Every note ends with an ask.

Never

  • "Trusted partner", "world-class", "leverage", "synergy", "solutions".
  • Exclamation marks. Emojis in documents (WhatsApp chat is fine).
  • Photos of ships, globes, or handshakes.
  • Colour used for emphasis. Emphasis is a shorter sentence.
